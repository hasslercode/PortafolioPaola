## Coca-Cola in the feed: global brand, local execution

Coca-Cola arrives at any point of sale with recognition few mass-consumer products can match. That is an advantage — and a risk on social. If content looks like recycled TV spot, audiences scroll silently. This case was about **translating a global brand into local, consumable moments on TikTok and Reels**, inside the Parque Alegra retail ecosystem in Barranquilla, without losing flavor, refreshment and sharing cues.

The featured piece associated with this experience reached **1.33 million organic views** — a documented number, not a projection. That volume does not come from logo magic; it comes when format respects how people consume short video in Colombia: vertical, fast, sensory and emotionally close.

## Context: tenant inside Parque Alegra

This experience was developed **within Parque Alegra**, a Parque Arauco shopping center in Barranquilla. Coca-Cola, present at point of sale, is part of the retail activation ecosystem documented in the general mall case — where campaigns together exceeded **1.3M+ organic views in 90 days**. The Coca-Cola piece was one of the most visible contributions to that total.

With the same transparency as the Parque Alegra case: **there is not always a direct contract between the content creator and the global brand**. The work reflects content produced for in-location consumption experience, with measurable organic results on the published piece.

## The problem: from brand icon to shareable moment

Beverage brands compete where everyone has cold product, bubbles and an iconic bottle. Organic differentiation lives in **the moment**: Barranquilla heat, pause between shopping, opening gesture, first sip, laughter with friends. The problem was avoiding advertising cliché — big logo, generic slogan, stock music — and building a sequence that triggered sensory desire in under fifteen seconds.

In mass consumption, algorithms reward what feels like recommendation or lived experience, not what feels like an ad. Coca-Cola already has awareness; what was missing was **feed-native piece** connecting product with real mall context.

## Process and results

The workflow followed four beats: **moment concept** (sequence, not bottle alone), **on-location capture** inside the establishment, **vertical editing** with retention pace and minimal on-screen text, and **publishing with tracking** of organic view accumulation.

The featured piece reached **1.33M views**, publicly verifiable. Qualitatively:

- Narrative felt **approachable and consumable**, not institutional.
- Vertical format showed global brands can adapt to feed language without losing identity.
- Case delivers **transferable social proof** for consumer brands in Colombian retail.

Not every Coca-Cola — or any big brand — piece replicates that volume. Timing, initial distribution and algorithm factors matter. What is replicable is the **method**: defined moment, real location, paced edit, publishing with follow-up.

## Lessons for consumer and retail brands

If you sell beverage, snack, beauty or any aisle SKU, the lesson is not copying Coca-Cola red. It is copying **discipline**: one emotion per piece, one clear hook, agile production at point of sale or real use, and honesty about which metric matters for your goal. Brands that win organically do not post more; they post **better** — with commercial intent and native format.
