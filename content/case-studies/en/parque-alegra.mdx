## Why a shopping mall needs organic content, not just storefronts

Parque Alegra in Barranquilla — operated by Parque Arauco — brings consumer, fashion, entertainment and food brands into one physical ecosystem. That is a huge advantage for visitors, but on social media the competition is not only the mall next door: it is the infinite scroll of TikTok, Reels and Shorts that captures attention before anyone walks through the door.

The challenge was not a generic architecture reel or a monthly “what’s new” carousel. It was to **activate specific tenants** — Coca-Cola, Starbucks, H&M, TOTTO, Cine Colombia and other mall experiences — with vertical pieces that felt native to the feed, sparked conversation and proved that physical retail can translate into shareable digital moments without relying on paid media alone.

## Transparency about the work context

Before the process, it is worth being explicit: **the brands mentioned here are establishments inside Parque Alegra**. There is not always a direct contractual relationship between Paola Hoyos and each global or local brand. The work is framed as **content for retail experiences within the mall ecosystem**, with measurable organic results across the campaign set. That honesty does not reduce the case value; it reflects how much retail content works in Colombia — in-store activations, location-linked pieces and narratives that connect product with local context.

If you are looking for a single brand with a closed annual retainer, this is not that format. If you want to see **how multi-million organic reach was built in 90 days** inside a real retail environment with known brands and replicable formats, this is it.

## The problem: fragmented visibility in a saturated feed

Each tenant has its own identity, marketing team (or none) and commercial calendar. Unifying them under one corporate mall voice often produces correct but forgettable content. Letting every brand post without format discipline creates inconsistency: a well-edited vertical Reel next to a cropped horizontal photo the algorithm downranks.

The core problem was **turning physical experiences — a coffee cup, a movie opening, an outfit, a consumer product — into short stories with hook, pace and closure**, published on platforms where retention is won in the first two seconds, with enough volume and cadence to accumulate organic views over time rather than depend on one isolated viral hit.

## Objective: sustained organic reach

The goal was clear: **organic visibility and engagement** with readable weekly metrics. That meant choosing formats Colombian audiences already consume — vertical, dynamic, on-screen text when needed — and adapting storytelling to each category without losing visual coherence.

Paid media for the mall or brands was not replaced; it was **complemented** with organic social proof that could be reused, cited and transferred as a reference for other retail activations in Colombia.

## Process: from brief to publishing with metric reading

Each piece started from a light but concrete brief: which product or experience to highlight, which emotion to trigger, which brand constraints to respect and which platform to prioritize. In retail, the brief cannot stay abstract — store hours, filming permissions and time-of-day light all matter.

Storytelling was built **on location**, not in a studio disconnected from the product. That means capturing store atmosphere, consumption gestures, packaging or texture details and human context that makes content believable. The point of sale is part of the hook: “this is what it feels like here, at Parque Alegra.”

Production prioritized agility over slow perfection — mobile capture, retention-paced editing, subtitles when audio was not the hero, durations tuned for TikTok and Instagram Reels. After publishing, tracking went beyond likes to **organic views, relative retention and patterns of pieces that sustained versus one-peak spikes**.

## Results: 1.3M+ organic views in 90 days

Across **90 days**, campaigns accumulated **more than 1.3 million organic views** across the ecosystem portfolio. That number is not a promise for every brand in every category — consistency, product, timing and algorithm luck all matter — but it documents real volume in Colombian retail context.

Beyond the total, the case shows three transferable points:

1. **Multi-brand content in one ecosystem** can add reach without pieces competing if calendar and format discipline exist.
2. **Organic social proof** — public views on profiles audiences can verify — weighs more than internal decks with decontextualized screenshots.
3. The **method** (brief → location → vertical → metrics) scales to SMBs and personal brands with controlled budgets, even when absolute numbers vary.

Known brands such as Coca-Cola, Starbucks, H&M, TOTTO and Cine Colombia participated, each with distinct organic behavior on featured pieces. Individual brand cases go deeper; this document closes the **Parque Alegra ecosystem** arc.
